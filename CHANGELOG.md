# Changelog

All changes compared to the original Castle Story game. Every entry has one tag:
`[ADD]` new feature, `[CHANGE]` changed behaviour, `[FIX]` bug fix, `[REMOVE]` removed feature.

## Unreleased

- [ADD] **Unity 6 port, routes over the navigation mesh (off by default):** with `[Pathfinding] NavMeshRoutes` (and `NavMeshExperiment` for the mesh) the route searches of the player's own units are answered by Unity's navigation mesh in one go (0.3 ms) instead of the game's A\* spread over many frames: the mesh's corners are turned back into the voxels the walk needs (a straight line between corners, each voxel a branch of the graph the unit's own search would take, links played as the graph's climb, step down and ladder branches, a blocked stretch mended over the graph), handed over as the search's result, so unrolling, straight walking and Locomotion4 are unchanged. Enemies, units that break their way and any route the mesh cannot give keep the A\*. On the benchmark save the main thread's search time fell from 2.6 s to 0.2-0.6 s per 150 s, routes are there after 2 frames instead of 6-14 (median), 3-10% of the requests went to the A\*, no unit got stuck. DevTools: `POST /api/navmesh/routes`, `routes` in `/api/navmesh`, outcome `navmesh` in `/api/path/stats`, tests `NavMeshRouteTests`. See docs/NavMesh.md.
- [CHANGE] **Navigation mesh experiment:** ground under buildings is left out of the mesh (routes no longer run along a building's very edge); doors get an area per owning faction, and a unit's query takes only the doors of its own, allied and neutral factions; a stockpile's pallets are an area of their own at a higher cost (walkable only while empty); buildings, doors and other obstacles placed, built or removed update the mesh (they change no terrain, so nothing did before); a link that reaches into another chunk (a climb, a ladder up a wall) is made again when that chunk changes, so a ladder whose top changed no longer keeps a stale link at its bottom.
- [FIX] **DevTools:** the developer plugin for the original game compiles again (no tuples, `Path.Combine` with two parts, the canvas alpha without the inherited part on Unity 5.6).
- [FIX] **Build:** the plugin for the original game compiles again; the tunnel goal refresh used `ConditionalWeakTable`, which .NET 3.5 does not have, and now keeps its passes in a table that holds a tunnel only while its pass is under way.
- [CHANGE] **Unity 6 port, pallet animation off the main thread:** when a storage's content changes, its pallet animation (the goods dropping into place, the heap rising) is no longer stepped by hand on the main thread every frame for a second (`Animator.Update`, about 0.4 ms a call); its animator is switched on for that time and Unity's animation pass works it out on its worker threads with the same time steps, then it is switched off again. About 0.36 ms a frame less on the main thread in a big base. `[Performance] ProgressionOnWorkers` (on by default) switches it off.
- [ADD] **Unity 6 port, jobs of our own:** the port's player carries an assembly of jobs for the mod (`CastleStoryPlus.Compute`), compiled by Burst when the player is built (`Plugins/lib_burst_generated.so`, also in the Mono player). The faction vision's detection step (which tracking groups a faction sees fully or partly) runs as such a job on Unity's worker threads, with the same results as before (checked against the game's sums with `CSP_COMPUTE_VERIFY=1`); `[Performance] PlayerJobs` switches it off. A player built before has no jobs and the work stays on the main thread. `PlayerBuild.LinuxTo` builds a player into the folder in `CSP_PLAYER_OUT`; every player build removes the note a build without Burst code left behind, which otherwise kept Unity from ever running Burst's step again.
- [ADD] **Performance window (F7):** the faction vision pass's steps are timed one by one (`vision:` rows).
- [ADD] **Navigation mesh experiment (Unity 6 port, off by default):** `[Pathfinding] NavMeshExperiment` builds Unity's navigation mesh from the game's route graph (a quad per place a bricktron stands on, links for climbing, stepping down and ladders, areas for forest, buildings and doors) on Unity's worker threads, and rebuilds the tiles round every terrain change. Nothing walks by it yet. DevTools `/api/navmesh` measures the build and the updates and compares its routes with the game's A\*: on the benchmark save a route takes 0.2 ms instead of 9 ms, is a fifth shorter, and agrees with the whole graph on reachability 99% of the time (the game's A\* 94%). See docs/NavMesh.md.
- [CHANGE] **Straight walks (Unity 6 port):** bricktrons walk straight lines across flat open ground instead of the route graph's voxel-by-voxel staircase (the graph links a voxel only to its four sides, so a slanted walk was an L or a zigzag). After the route search, runs of plain flat steps are pulled tight onto the voxels a straight line passes through; a line is only taken over route nodes at one height and surface, with plain steps between them, no door, obstacle or unit the route search would avoid on it, and flat open ground beside it for the bricktron's body (no cut corners, no walking along a drop). Climbing, steps down, ladders, stairs, forest and doors stay as the search found them. The route still holds every voxel walked, so the game's own checks while walking (who stands in the next voxel, doors, ground changed under the route, repaths) work as before; the bricktron only aims along the line. On a test walk of 14 x 6 voxels it strayed at most 0.23 voxel from the line (2.8 to 3.6 before) and walked 15.4 voxels instead of 16.7 to 17.8. `[Pathfinding] StraightPaths` (on by default). DevTools: `GET /api/path/straight?from=&to=` tells whether a straight walk passes the line test and where it fails; `/api/path/stats` counts the straightened routes; in-game tests `StraightPathTests` (straight walk, pillar on the line, wall built on the line and a pit dug on it while walking).
- [FIX] **MCP:** `game_screenshot` sends the picture at most 1280 pixels wide, so an MCP client with a 10 MB message limit no longer drops the connection on a large screenshot (a 2560 px PNG of the world was 5.8 MB, a third more as base64). `POST /api/screenshot` takes `maxWidth` and writes a scaled copy next to the full-size file, scaled on the graphics card on every platform. Reported and first solved in the MCP server by Pabloks-png (pull request #4).
- [FIX] **SmartTransfer:** right-clicking a stockpile with selected bricktrons no longer leaves them standing idle at it for ever: they store what they carry there, or pick up an item of the stockpile that fits. The game handed the pickup the stockpile itself, whose access handling never completes, so the worker's current SmartTransfer task never ended and the stop key only queued another order. A SmartTransfer order that has left a worker standing idle for 10 seconds ends by itself.
- [FIX] **Settings window:** the Castle Story Plus settings window fits on the screen: its settings scroll under the title, which stays put, and 720 px of them show at once. The window grew with every setting, so on a 1440p or 1080p screen the keys, feature switches and notes at the bottom ran past the window's edge and could not be reached.
- [ADD] **Renderer choice (Unity 6 port, Linux):** choose OpenGL Core or Vulkan in Settings > Castle Story Plus settings (`[Graphics] Renderer`). The game starts again with the chosen API (saved first when it is restarted from the settings), also when it was started from Steam or a shortcut; if the chosen API cannot start, the choice goes back to the default (OpenGL Core). The Linux player is built with both APIs.
- [CHANGE] **Unity 6 port, Vulkan:** a player built with both Vulkan and OpenGL Core gets every converted shader for both at install (as Unity itself writes such a shader), so one install starts with either `-force-glcore` or `-force-vulkan`; `CSP_VULKAN=1` is no longer needed. A shader whose Vulkan programs cannot all be made keeps its OpenGL programs, and the install log says so. The content folder gets `graphics.txt`, the graphics APIs the shaders were converted for (Vulkan only when every shader has it). Takes effect when the content is converted again.
- [FIX] **Unity 6 port, Vulkan:** the crystals rendered wrong (shards seemingly moved or turned, black facets, grey shapes where the wall behind showed through). The converted shaders' position lost precision going through OpenGL's depth range and back, so whole triangles failed the depth test; the position now comes from Unity's own Vulkan matrix, and forward passes keep a tiny depth margin. Takes effect when the content is converted again.
- [CHANGE] **Faster faction vision:** the vision pass (who sees which units and items) no longer goes over every tracking group of the map once for each bricktron and building. The visions are put in buckets by place, a bucket settles most groups at once, and only the groups at its edge are tested vision by vision with the game's own sums, so the same units are seen at the same moments. Its worst frame on a big base was up to 9 ms. Part of `FrameSavers`.
- [CHANGE] **Workers looking for work:** before a worker takes a task, the game checks for each kind of thing the task gives or needs whether there is a storage for it or one to fetch; it went on checking after the answer was already decided, and since the faster task search all of it ran in one frame (up to several ms per asking worker). It now stops at the deciding answer, with the same result. Harvest tasks count their goals with a plain loop. Part of `FrameSavers`.
- [CHANGE] **Smaller per-frame savings:** overhead icons read the camera's position once per frame instead of each on its own; a unit's way of carrying (hands, back, bag) is remembered per carried thing instead of looked up through the factory every frame while it walks; storage fill animations wait while the storage is off the screen and catch up when it comes into view; mouse picking casts each identical ray once per frame; the job board builds its counter names only while measuring. Part of `FrameSavers`.
- [ADD] **Performance window (F7):** with `[Performance] MeasureAllocations` the labor tree's sequences and kinds of work also show what they allocate.
- [CHANGE] **Fewer objects for the garbage collector:** a game signal that nothing has connected to yet no longer keeps two empty lists of its own. A big base had some 700 000 of them, a ninth of all objects on the managed heap, and every garbage collection looks at each one, so the pauses get shorter. Part of `FrameSavers`.
- [FIX] **Unity 6 port:** the content converter leaves out the components whose script the original game had already lost (`StorageLocator`, `Construction`, `Mine` and other factory templates, a few menu objects). Each new task logged a warning with a stack trace for them. Takes effect when the content is converted again.
- [ADD] **Performance window (F7):** also times the end of a route search (`path: ConcludeSearch`, turning the coarse route into a fine one), its start and copying the result to the worker, so a slow frame in a worker's walk shows where the time went.
- [ADD] **Code style:** the code style is checked on every pull request (`.github/workflows/code-style.yml`), and a pull request can only be merged when it passes: braces on every block, Allman braces, tab indentation and file-scoped namespaces (`.editorconfig`, with `dotnet format`), 24-hour time and day- or year-first dates. `tools/check-style.sh` runs the same check locally, `--fix` fixes the layout. The code was brought in line (switch cases indented in 32 files, one brace added).
- [FIX] **Debug menu:** dropping many resources at once (for example 50) no longer sends them flying in all directions. They were all spawned in one point, and the physics engine pushed the overlapping items apart at high speed. Each now gets its own place in a square of up to 8 x 8 (more layers stacked over it), and the physics may push a spawned item out of another only slowly (1 m/s instead of 10). The same goes for `POST /api/cheat/items`.
- [ADD] **Performance window (F7):** the header shows the graphics API the game renders with (OpenGL Core, Vulkan or Direct3D 11), whether rendering is multithreaded, and the graphics card.
- [ADD] **Why a blueprint is not built:** hovering a blueprint that is not being built shows why, in a small panel over the cursor: its task is not committed, paused (locked) or has no bricktrons, something is in the way (named, and the cells it stands in are marked red in the world: the ground or a block, a tree, a stockpile's goods, a bricktron, a building or another blueprint), it waits for what it rests on, or no bricktron can get to a place to build it from (it needs stairs, a ramp, a ladder or a floor next to it). A blueprint waiting for support also tells how many of its task's blueprints no bricktron can get to. Nothing shows while a blueprint can be built.
- [ADD] **License:** the mod's code is licensed under the GNU General Public License v3 or later (`LICENSE`): free to use, copy, change and share; changed versions that are shared must come with their full source code under the same license and keep the copyright notice (`NOTICE`). `NOTICE` adds a permission to combine the mod with Castle Story, Unity and BepInEx. The release packages carry both files. Castle Story itself is not covered.
- [FIX] **Stacking:** a full stockpile no longer sticks through a floor built over it. With the Stacking research a full stockpile piles twice as high as the game's (some 3.8 blocks over the bottom of its cell instead of 1.9), but it kept only its own 2 blocks free. Now what is over a stockpile decides: its room is cut so a full stockpile reaches no higher than the first block, plank floor, beam or building over it (a slab floor right over the goods of a full stockpile can be built), and a block or other blueprint cannot be placed where its goods stand now. Over an empty or low stockpile you can still build, and its room then shrinks to what fits; goods already higher stay until taken.
- [ADD] **DevTools:** `GET /api/storage` also lists each resource's room and the top of what a storage draws.
- [ADD] **Job board:** an idle worker remembers, per task, an ask for work that came to nothing, and does not ask that task again until something it depends on changed (the task's goals or crew, the worker's autonomy, class, bag or place, the route graph, and for a storage failure any stockpile) or 1 to 5 seconds passed. Idle workers asked every task every 2 ticks and paid each time for the storage check and the search through all goals, only to be turned down again; about three of four such asks are now skipped and the workers' search costs some 40 % less per tick. The list of tasks, their order and every rule of the search stay the game's; the idle task and raids are always asked. `[JobBoard] JobBoardMode`: On (default), Shadow (asks as before and only counts, in the performance window, what would be skipped and what would have been skipped wrongly) or Off.
- [CHANGE] **Frame savers:** an icon sorted by its distance to the camera sets its canvas's sorting order only when it changed and only while it shows (some 350 icons set it every frame).
- [ADD] **DevTools:** `POST /api/components?q=Type&enabled=false|true|restore` switches live components of a type off and on, to measure what they cost.
- [ADD] **Unity 6 port:** experimental Vulkan for the Linux player (`CSP_VULKAN=1` at install, `PlayerBuild.VulkanTestLinux`), so the player records its draw calls on several threads (native graphics jobs; OpenGL cannot). The SPIR-V the 5.6 game has for 144 of its 179 shaders is repacked for Unity 6; the other 35 are rewritten from their OpenGL programs and compiled with shaderc, which the installer ships. A test island renders as on OpenGL. Not the default yet.
- [ADD] **DevTools:** `GET /api/frames/markers` (Unity 6) reads Unity's own profiler markers per frame (culling, drawing, shadows, skinning, animation, physics), all of them in the port's development player, to split up the engine's time that the player loop steps cannot.
- [CHANGE] **Faster task search:** the search for the nearest thing holding a resource (a builder's material, a workshop's ingredient) and for the nearest stockpile with room tests first whether a thing holds the resource or has room, and only then whether the faction can see it. The sight test is the costly one (a good on a pallet asks the pallet's sight in turn) and was run for every reachable thing of every resource: a material search took 1-3 ms at once, now some 0.4 ms. Same answer as before.
- [ADD] **Performance window (F7):** the knowledge searches' parts are measured too (reachable things, sight, stockpile contents, exclusions).
- [CHANGE] **Unreachable tasks:** the workers' reach map is flooded for at most 0.4 ms a frame (was 4000 places a frame, about 0.8 ms) and at most every 5 seconds after the world changed (was every second, so a quarry being dug kept a flood running in nearly every frame).
- [CHANGE] **Ore mine:** each pass skips the ore cells that already have a goal and, for 30 seconds, the ones the full rules turned down (no place to stand, not safe to dig); every pass checked them all again, which kept the passes of two mines at some 0.8 ms a frame.
- [CHANGE] **Worker AI:** a worker that has looked for work in vain for 3 seconds asks the projects every 3rd round, after 15 seconds every 5th (about the game's own pace); a worker coming off a task asks every round as before.
- [ADD] **DevTools:** `POST /api/render?instancing=true|false` switches GPU instancing on the mesh renderers' materials, for measuring.
- [FIX] **Ranged reposition:** archers and other ranged fighters on a wall no longer climb or drop down to fight an enemy at the foot of the wall. A fighter holding its place still looked for an enemy within 2 blocks to fight hand to hand, but the game measured only the horizontal distance; the search now also stays within 1 block up or down for a player's ranged fighter holding its place.
- [CHANGE] **DevTools:** the API runs at most 2 ms of requests a frame on the game's main thread (a burst of requests froze the game for a second).
- [FIX] **Worker AI:** workers no longer dig, harvest or clean up what they cannot store. The game took such a goal when any one of the things it yields had room: a clay or ore voxel also gives stones, so with room for stones and the clay pallets full a worker dug anyway and was left carrying clay it could put nowhere (too full to dig, walking between storages, dropping it at last for the next worker to pick up). Now every thing the goal yields needs room in the worker's bag and a storage.
- [FIX] **Pathfinding:** bricktrons no longer get stuck jumping between two cells at a quarry's rim. The route graph could keep a node in a voxel that had since become solid ground; routes led into it, the game pushed the bricktron out of the ground and blocked its path, and the next route led straight back, until the game was restarted. When the game pushes a bricktron out of a voxel that still holds a route node, that node is now rebuilt from the terrain.
- [ADD] **DevTools:** `GET /api/path/occupied` shows why a unit would be pushed out of a voxel: whether it is open, nature blockers and the volumes there.
- [ADD] **Frame savers:** outside a running game (main menu, lobby, while a world loads) the frame rate is held at most at 300 frames a second (`[Performance] MenuFpsCap`, 0: no cap). The menus draw next to nothing, so without vsync they ran at thousands of frames a second (some 5000 for a moment while a world loads), which only heats the graphics card and makes it whine. In a game the player's own limit applies as before, and saving the options in the menu keeps the player's limit, not the cap.
- [FIX] **DevTools:** `POST /api/quit` closes the game through the game's own quit (with the mod: a save still being written finishes, then the process ends) instead of Unity's `Application.Quit`, which sometimes left the Linux player running after a save and quit.
- [CHANGE] **Frame savers:** whether an object is gone (asked by every working bricktron's task each tick and by the task search for every item, storage and goal it weighs) no longer looks up the object's game component every time: the component is remembered per object, and its released and destroyed flags are still read each time, so the answer is the same.
- [ADD] **Performance window (F7):** the workers' behaviour tree is measured in parts: its five sequences (current work, pending work, looking for work, autonomy, voxel presence), the current work per kind of task, the projects' work checks, the work conditions checked each tick, path following, the enemy watch and the wait to stand still.
- [FIX] **Performance window (F7):** a measured method called on one of the game's worker threads (terrain, routes) no longer touches the monitor's bucket lists there; such a call had killed the terrain's LOD thread.
- [CHANGE] **Frame savers:** a unit's movement each frame (`Locomotion4.ApplyAnimatorMotion`) no longer goes through a hidden helper transform: the levelled facing, the floor height and the snap are worked out on plain values, and the unit's rotation is set only when it changed and its position only when it moved (some 13 fewer engine calls per moving unit a frame). Climbing, stepping down, ladders, jumping, falling and every refused or blocked move still use the game's own code.
- [FIX] **Game leak fixes:** the in-game selection list (`UIGameSelector`) never removed anything (the game's unregister test was inverted), so it kept every worker, block, building and project of the session, destroyed ones included, and carried them into the menu and the next game; a heap scan counted some 66 MB behind it. Released objects are now removed, destroyed ones are dropped as the list grows, and the list is emptied when a game is left.
- [FIX] **Game leak fixes:** the distant-terrain (LOD) array pool kept the peak of the first full LOD build (about 50 MB) for the whole game. It is now trimmed to 8 MB after a large LOD build; small updates keep what they need.
- [FIX] **Game leak fixes:** clearing a world emptied the LOD pools but also forgot them, so from the second world of a session they were never emptied again and kept the previous world's LOD arrays. They are now emptied every time.
- [ADD] **Market:** the market's window remembers the trades made before: the last 6 queued and up to 8 favourites are listed over the goods. A click chooses that trade's goods, "Fav" marks it as a favourite (kept at the top, not pushed out by newer trades) and "x" takes it out of the list. The lists are kept in the settings (`[Market] RecentTrades`, `FavouriteTrades`), the same at every market and in every save.
- [CHANGE] **Build shortage box:** the icons over waiting blueprints are only redrawn when a count changes them, a blueprint is finished or the camera moves, and the box only rewrites what changed. The resource list and the camera are looked up at most twice a second, and the counts reuse their working sets. Same display, much less work and garbage every frame (it cost some 0.2 ms a frame).
- [CHANGE] **Unity 6 port:** the game scene's second EventSystem is now switched off in the port's player too (before only in the Unity 6 editor), so only one EventSystem gets an update each frame.
- [FIX] Distant terrain (LOD) redraws no longer stop with an "index out of range" in `Display.CountLocalizedMesh` when the terrain worker thread fails part way: unfinished sections keep their previous mesh and the rest is drawn (the game drew from arrays the failed thread never filled, and the aborted redraw did not return its pooled arrays).
- [FIX] **Unity 6 port:** digging a quarry no longer floods the log with "Mesh Collider ... doesn't have any vertices": empty terrain sections get no collider mesh instead of an empty one (the same collision as before).
- [CHANGE] **Ore mine:** the search for the ore a mine's crew may dig runs on another thread. The mine keeps its own copy of the voxels around its sphere (a byte a cell, some 100 KB for a radius of 20), filled once a slice per frame and again every 2 minutes, and kept up to date in between from the game's voxel change signals; every 5 seconds a pool thread goes over the sphere in the copy, and the main thread only checks the cells it found with the full rules (the crew's reach map, trees and boulders) before it makes a goal. Before, the main thread went over the whole sphere itself every pass, some 0.5 ms a frame with two mines.
- [ADD] **Performance window (F7):** "FPS counter at the top" shows a small counter at the top centre of the screen: frames per second and frame time over the last half second, yellow under 60 fps and red under 30. It stays on with the window closed and is remembered (`[Debug] FpsCounter`).
- [CHANGE] **Performance window (F7):** opening the window no longer starts measuring the systems: patching them stopped the game for some 5 seconds, and the measuring cost about 1.5 ms a frame for the rest of the game, so the frame rate the window showed was lower than without it. The header's "Measure systems" button starts it and now also stops it again (`POST /api/frames/stop` too). The systems now also include the Updates of the libraries the game ships with (networking, UI, input, camera, its UnityScript parts) and of the mod's other assemblies, which counted as unmeasured before.
- [CHANGE] **Faster task search:** whether a worker could take a resource and store it (room in its bag and a storage with room) was worked out again for every block of a quarry or dig area it weighed, though the answer is the same for every block of that resource. With the storage full every block failed that check, and a search through a large quarry spent several milliseconds learning the same thing hundreds of times. The answer is now kept for the rest of the frame, per worker and resource.
- [CHANGE] **TaskPrediction:** in a quarry the prediction often gave up: the game's choice compares every block (a reach check and a check for storage each), and in a large quarry that took more than the 4 ms one prediction may take, so the worker stood waiting for the game's slow search again. The blocks are now checked in the order of the priority the game gives them (highest layer first, then the nearest; blocks marked unreachable last), so the first block the worker can dig is the game's choice and the search ends there. A worker that took what it dug or felled to storage (a task the game gives on its own) also goes straight back to its area's next task; before, only the area's own tasks were followed by a prediction. The worker trace gives how many tasks a prediction checked and how long it took.
- [FIX] **Performance window (F7):** some measured methods also run on the game's worker threads (route searches, terrain), and their calls broke the main thread's measuring stack: an IndexOutOfRangeException in FrameMonitor.End and times of hours for some systems. Only calls on the main thread are measured now.
- [FIX] **Game leak fixes:** the game sent its statistics (GameAnalytics) to a server that no longer answers. Every 10 seconds the request failed ("GA Error: Cannot connect to destination host"), the failed events stayed queued to go again with the next ones, and every other attempt made some 21 MB of garbage in one frame, more the longer the game ran. Nothing is queued or sent any more (`AnalyticsOff`).
- [ADD] **Performance window (F7) / DevTools:** frames that allocate 4 MB or more without a garbage collection are kept as allocation spikes, with the systems that allocated the most in them (`GET /api/frames`: `allocSpikes`).
- [CHANGE] **UnreachableTasks:** the workers' reachability map is flooded again a second after every change of the world (all the time while workers dig), and each flood made a new queue, visited set and map of up to some 150 000 places, growing and rehashing as it went: about half the garbage a running game made (49 KB a frame of 110). A flood now reuses its queue and visited set, and each faction keeps two maps, the one in use and the one being flooded.
- [ADD] **DevTools:** `POST /api/quit?save=true` saves the running game first, as the quit menu's save and quit does (the loaded manual save overwritten, else a new save), so a restart keeps the game; nothing closes when the save fails.
- [ADD] **DevTools:** `GET /api/frames/loop` (Unity 6) also gives what each of Unity's player loop steps allocates (`kbPerFrame`; `sort=kb`), to find allocations outside the scripts the frame monitor measures.
- [CHANGE] **Game leak fixes:** every adjective set (`DicoAdjectif`: what a thing is, holds or may hold) made two dictionaries (its field's initializer and then its constructor's) and a StringWriter with its StringBuilder and buffer, which is used only when Unity serializes the set. Descriptions make new sets in all their sums and differences, many times a second, and a heap scan found some 70 000 alive. A set now makes only its constructor's dictionary, and its StringWriter the first time it is serialized: fewer allocations, and fewer objects for each garbage collection to go through.
- [FIX] **Unity 6 port:** bricktrons could not climb up or step down a block. They played the climb on the spot again and again and could not get out of a quarry. Both moves reach their goal by the animator's target matching, and the game applies the animator's motion itself (root motion off, no `OnAnimatorMove`). Unity 5.6 matched the target that way; Unity 6 started the match but never moved the unit towards it. Every unit now gets an empty `OnAnimatorMove` (`TargetMatchFix`), so the root motion counts as handled by a script: Unity 6 matches the target again, and the game still applies the motion itself.
- [FIX] **Unity 6 port:** what bricktrons carry and hold sat in the wrong place, for example a sack in front of the belly instead of on the back. The grip and socket matrices (`Grip.gripMatrix`, `GripSocket.socketMatrix`) were identity: the installer described a script's `Matrix4x4` field by its C# field names (`m00`, ...), while Unity writes it as `Matrix4x4f` with `e00` to `e33`, so Unity 6 matched no field. The installer now uses Unity's names; install again to get it.
- [ADD] **DevTools:** `GET /api/components` takes `path` (only objects whose path holds it) and `fields=true` (the values of the components' serialized fields and their local transform), to compare what the game's assets hold on 5.6 and in the port.
- [FIX] **Unity 6 port:** no map could be started from the new game screen: selecting one threw in `Minimap.GetTerrainBounds` ("Texture2D.GetPixels32: invalid mipmap level"), its details stayed placeholder labels and Play did nothing. The game reads the map's heightmap at mipmap level 3; Unity 6 loads that image without mipmaps. Without the level, the bounds are now worked out from the full-size image.
- [ADD] **Debug menu (F8):** "Instant build" turns on the game's own editor mode: what you place is built at once, without workers or materials, and what you remove goes at once. It is the same switch as the game's debug sidebar, which only shows on debug maps or with the game's debug menu allowed. It is off again when the game is left.
- [FIX] **Debug menu (F8) and performance window (F7):** they no longer reach past the bottom of the screen. The game's UI is always 1080 units tall, and the F7 window was 1260; the F8 window grew past the screen as sections were added. What is under their headers now scrolls when they are taller than the screen.
- [ADD] **Unity 6 port (in progress):** Castle Story on a current Unity (6000.6), made at install time from the player's own Castle Story install; nothing of the game is distributed. `CastleStoryPlus.Player` is a Unity 6 player with no game content. `CastleStoryPlus.Port` turns the install into what the player loads. It checks every game file against a SHA-256 manifest (`game-files.sha256`) and does nothing unless all match. It patches the game's assemblies for Unity 6 with Cecil (removed engine types moved to `CastleStoryPlus.Compat`, renamed types and changed calls redirected). It repacks the game's 5.6 data files as asset bundles with type trees, which Unity 6 upgrades as it loads them, and copies the game's plugins and StreamingAssets.
