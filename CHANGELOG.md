# Changelog

All changes compared to the original Castle Story game. Every entry has one tag:
`[ADD]` new feature, `[CHANGE]` changed behaviour, `[FIX]` bug fix, `[REMOVE]` removed feature.

## 0.3.0 — 2026-10-06

- [ADD] **WaveInterval:** a new Invasion world can have its enemy waves come every 5, 10 (default), 15, 20 or 30 minutes: "Waves every" under the difficulty on the new game screen (the host chooses). It sets the game's own wave time, which is saved with the world; the first wave keeps the difficulty's extra head start.
- [FIX] **StockpileSpill:** stones, ore, coal, brimstone and crystals dropped from a demolished stockpile sank into the ground after half a minute: the game merges loose gravel back into the terrain (meant for what digging leaves). Dropped stockpile contents now stay until picked up. Each item also gets a place of its own on the open ground round the stockpile, at most 8 to a block, instead of many on one spot.
- [FIX] **Warehouse:** its blueprint could not be turned while placing: it is made from the stockpile's, which has turning locked (a stockpile is the same every way round). Custom buildings now turn all four ways.
- [CHANGE] **BuildJobs:** a worker fetching material for a blueprint takes as much as it can carry, up to what that blueprint and the nearest other blueprints of the same project (within 12 blocks) still need, and delivers to them one after the other on the same trip; a row of stockpiles gets three logs from one worker instead of one each. What workers bring is now counted in items, not workers, so others go to the blueprints that are not covered yet.
- [FIX] **CustomBlocks:** dragging a row of the long blocks (2x4 bricks and slabs) placed them overlapping by one block: the game's drag stepped by 3 for a block 4 long. They now follow each other exactly, in every direction and turned either way.
- [ADD] **StockpileTooltip:** a warehouse shows how full it is in percent of its room ("Warehouse 82% full"); its resources take different room each (a brick as much as 20 stones).
- [FIX] **AutoCleanup:** idle workers never picked up weapons, armour and other items the workers' knowledge does not track: asking it whether such an item can be reached always says no. Those are now tried, and an item a worker set out for and did not get stored is left alone for 2 minutes.
- [FIX] **StockpileTooltip:** a stored weapon or tool also showed as "1 Resource" (the game's tool property).
- [FIX] **Warehouse:** "You need more empty stockpiles!" showed while a warehouse still had room: the game counts only empty storages. A warehouse with room left now counts as one, since it takes any mix of resources.
- [FIX] **CustomBlocks:** large wood slabs (2x2, 2x4) and the large bricks were never built when the builders could only reach them from the blocks beside them, e.g. a walkway of slabs along the top of a wall: they had no places to stand one level higher. They now have them, as the game's own brick does (beside, below, one higher and two lower, up to the 32 places the workers keep per block).
- [ADD] **Developer tools** (not part of releases): unit tests of the mod's logic (`CastleStoryPlus.Tests`), a developer plugin with an HTTP API on localhost into the running game and in-game tests of the pathfinding (`CastleStoryPlus.DevTools`), and an MCP server that gives Claude Code the API (`CastleStoryPlus.Mcp`). The quarry depth steps and the world search text were moved into small classes of their own so they can be tested.
- [ADD] **WorldSearch:** Ctrl+F opens a search bar at the top of the screen. Everything in the world matching the text gets a yellow ring under it, also through walls: bricktrons by name, job or kind (enemies too), buildings, blueprints and resources by type. Several searches can be separated by commas; case does not matter. The rings follow moving units and are kept up to date while the bar is open. Escape or X closes it. While typing, the game's keys stand by.
- [FIX] **CustomBlocks:** the wood slabs were never made: they waited for the plank's material, which the plank's prefab does not have (the game gives it one while playing). They are now made without it. Their pieces for the ghost and the placed slab had no width (invisible), and built slabs showed magenta: they now take the material the game draws placed planks with.
- [ADD] **QuarryDepth:** a quarry's depth can be changed at any time, also while it is being dug: a depth button in the quarry menu shows the depth in blocks, with small "+" and "-" over it (1 block at a time up to 10, then 5). The stairs are extended to the new bottom; the change is synced to multiplayer clients and saved with the quarry. Quarries can go 3 times as deep as before, by the buttons and by the drag plane; never below the bottom of the island.
- [FIX] **StockpileSpill:** a demolished stockpile or warehouse destroyed what it held. It now drops its contents on the ground, also when it is destroyed by enemies. The game looked for a free spot in only 4 places next to the stockpile, and put the items down a frame after they were already gone.
- [FIX] **QuarryLimit:** the small "+" and "-" over the quarry's resource buttons crashed the game menu's Lua while a map loaded, and much of the game UI was missing: the game's Lua engine breaks when a panel is handed straight from a chain of calls to the game; it is now kept in a variable first, as the game's own menus do. The buttons also failed to refresh the shown limit while the menu was open.
- [CHANGE] **FasterLoading:** when a Lua menu stops on an error while it loads, the log names the menu and its Lua call stack.
- [ADD] **Build menu icons:** the warehouse, the market, the smithy, the armoury and the new blocks have their own icons in the build menu, drawn in the style of the game's build icons (isometric, flat colours, dark outline) by `tools/make_build_icons.py` into `Assets/Build`. The game's icon stands in if a file is missing.
- [ADD] **CustomBlocks:** the key T (`[Building] BlockAnchorKey`) chooses which corner of a new brick or slab goes where the mouse points, each corner in turn, so a block need not be placed by its middle. The blocks also turn all four ways.
- [FIX] **CustomBlocks:** the new blocks had no ghost while placing and as a blueprint: the game draws a block's ghost (and its placed block) piece by piece from a mesh descriptor and gives the ghost shader each piece's position; without one the ghost stayed invisible. Each block now has a descriptor: the bricks reuse the game's brick's two pieces (with their hidden-face variants, so faces between blocks are not drawn), the slabs have one piece per block. The 2 x 4 brick was also placed half a block off: the game's brick fitter centres a 1 x 2 block on a block border; the new blocks have their own.
- [FIX] **Warehouse:** it could be placed over other buildings: the stockpile it is cloned from has a placement check that lets it overlap things. It now has the workbench's check, as the other mod buildings.
- [ADD] **CustomBlocks:** new blocks in the build menu's brick wheel. Stone: a large brick of 2 x 2 blocks (2 bricks) and a long brick of 2 x 4 (4 bricks), the game's brick mesh laid side by side, resting on stone or the ground like the game's brick. Wood: slabs half a block high (in the upper half of the block, like the game's plank, so they are walked on at the block's top) of 1 x 1, 2 x 1, 2 x 2 and 2 x 4 blocks (1, 1, 2 and 4 planks). A slab also holds on to the side of a stone block or the terrain, with nothing under it: one stone or ground cell below or round it at its own height is enough; a fallen slab is planks. Each block is cloned from the game's brick (the slab's free block from the plank) with IDs 9001 to 9006, which saves keep; saves with these blocks need the mod. The log lists the source cells for checking.
- [ADD] **Warehouse:** a warehouse in the build menu (stockpile group): a 9 x 6 hall of brick and timber with wide cart doors, a gable roof with a hoist, and an open brick works on the right. It holds 4500 resources of any kind, mixed (the stockpile's bulk units: 4500 stones, 225 bricks, 675 planks, ...), and workers walk in through the doors and store and fetch standing inside. Its brick works turns stored stone into bricks by itself, 20 stones to a brick, a few every 3 seconds while the stockpile's "convert to bricks" switch is on; the game's own conversion (fill a full stockpile with bricks, whatever else it holds) is off for it. It is a clone of the stockpile, so it has the stockpile's menu and resource filters, counts as storage everywhere (resource list, stock limits) and shows in the stockpile tooltip; stockpile consolidation leaves it alone. Cost: 60 planks, 80 bricks, 20 iron, 10 rope.
- [ADD] **DebugMenu** (modding aid): when the game has made its block types, every block is written to `BepInEx/blockdump.log`: ID, keys, groups, resource, size, HP, the blueprint's cost, components (fitter, validator) and cells, and the block's mesh, materials and shaders. These values live in the game's prefabs, not in code, and new blocks are cloned from them.
- [CHANGE] **Market / Upgrades:** workers walk into the market, the smithy and the armoury instead of stopping beside them: they deliver, fetch and work standing on the floor inside (between the market's shelves and counter, at the smithy's hearth and anvil, among the armoury's stands). The floor cells of a building no longer block the pathfinder (they still keep anything from being built or dropped inside), the places where workers stand are inside, and the game's ring of places round the outside is gone; walls, furniture and the hearth still block. Each building has a small floor plan for this (`CustomBuilding.Layout`), and its model is lowered so its floor is at the ground. The blueprints are unchanged, so builders still build from outside.
- [FIX] **Pathfinding:** bricktrons never used stairs that run under a walkway (e.g. steps under a wall walk). The game wants 2 empty voxels above a full surface and 1 above a slope voxel, and any block above counts whatever its shape, so the steps under the walkway lost their path nodes and the flight was cut. On stair blocks (the slope and its full step, normal, spiral, corner and single stairs) 1 empty voxel above the step's lowest point is now enough, both when a map loads and when blocks change; flat ground keeps the game's rule. The head may go a little into the walkway.
- [ADD] **StockpileTooltip:** hovering a stockpile (or a weapon stand or other storage that shows the game's icons and counts) shows a small panel next to the mouse with what it holds, one line per resource: icon, name and count, sorted by count, with the capacity when it holds a single resource (e.g. "Stone 120 / 240"), or "Empty". Mixed pallets list each resource. The game's own icons and counts above it stay.
- [FIX] **Metallurgy:** cleanup areas left loose coal lying: their workers walk to the loose items and take in passing only what goes in the bag (stones, raw iron, crystals, plants, bombs), a list coal was not on. Coal now goes in the bag like raw iron.
- [ADD] **DarkCrystals:** a new resource, dark crystal, that only enemies give: a slain corruptron, minitron or magitron drops 5 dark crystals with a 1 in 3 chance, a biftron 15 with a 1 in 2 chance. The crystal tier of every upgrade costs dark crystals instead of blue crystal (blue crystal is still used to brew fireflies), and a 3x bricktron costs 50 dark crystals (`[GiantBricktron] DarkCrystalCost`) instead of energy: the energy hardly ever built up, as the home crystal turns it into new bricktrons. Dark crystal is the game's unused resource type PurifiedBlueCrystal (the network and saves already know it), with its own item (the raw blue crystal turned purple and glowing blue: a blue emission and a small blue light; it never merges back into the terrain), icons (`tools/make_resource_icons.py`) and entries in the resource list and stockpiles. It is carried in the bag like blue crystal (so cleanup areas pick it up), and everything that can hold orange crystal can hold as much dark crystal. The debug menu can drop it. Drops happen on the host. Saves with dark crystals need the mod.
- [FIX] **Metallurgy:** coal veins were an exact copy of the iron veins and, coming after iron in the list, replaced all of them (the log's deep rock shares showed iron and coal at exactly the same share): the coal noise's new seeds only reached modules under a noise combiner, not under a selector or modifier, so most of the copied iron noise kept iron's seeds. The noise is now rebuilt from the leaves up through every kind of module, so coal veins lie elsewhere and iron is back. Only rock nobody has dug yet changes.
- [FIX] **StockpilePickup:** workers fetching from a stockpile failed when another worker got there first, although the stockpile still held plenty: the game sends a worker to one particular item, the first of its kind, so every worker fetching that kind from the stockpile went for the same item. Now a worker takes any item of the kind once it is at the stockpile, and fails only if none is left. The worker trace showed this as the most common reason for failed build tasks (48 of 55).
- [FIX] **TaskReservation:** a worker whose task for a goal failed, or was never started, kept the goal reserved for the full 15 seconds, and searching again often reserved it again, so other workers turned open build tasks down as "already enough workers" and stood idle. After 2 seconds a reservation now only holds while the worker's current or next task is that goal.
- [ADD] **PassThroughAllies:** bricktrons walk through each other: allies never block each other's path (enemies still do), so workers no longer wait for or walk around each other in doorways, on stairs and on narrow walls. The workers' reachability map also counted a voxel with a bricktron in it as a wall, so a worker in a doorway could make everything behind it "out of reach"; it now ignores bricktrons, as the game's other reachability map already does.
- [ADD] **QuarryLimit:** a quarry can dig each resource until the team has enough of it. In the quarry menu, small "+" and "-" buttons over each resource button (stone, iron, brimstone, coal, blue crystal) set a limit for that resource: none, then 5 at a time under 50, 10 under 200, then 50; the limit shows as a yellow number under the resource button, and the buttons' tooltip lists every limit with the stock. While the team's stockpiles hold that much or more (stone counted as bricks, raw stone 20 to a brick, as the stockpiles convert it), the quarry's workers leave blocks of that resource alone and dig the rest; they dig them again by themselves once the stock drops below the limit. The limits are per quarry, saved with it (an extra `resourceLimits` property, ignored by the unmodded game) and synced to multiplayer clients.
- [ADD] **Metallurgy / BlueCrystalDeposits:** quarries and tunnels have a "keep coal" and a "keep blue crystal" button next to the stone, iron and orange crystal buttons. Before, coal followed the iron setting and blue crystal was always kept. Both are kept by default, also in quarries and tunnels of older saves (they are stored as "off" flags in the project's resource setting, which the unmodded game ignores).
- [ADD] **WorkerTrace** (modding aid): the worker trace can now be switched on and off while playing, in the debug menu (F8), and explains idle workers: why each worker rejected tasks while looking for work (the game's decision errors such as too crowded, cannot reach, bag full, no storage, summed every 5 seconds), every task that ends with its result, error and duration, every failed path with its destination, every failed search for something to pick up (what was wanted, how much the stockpiles hold, whether a source could be found without the other workers' reservations, whether it fits in the bag, what the bag holds), and every 10 seconds the workers standing idle while their projects have open tasks.
- [FIX] **BlueCrystalDeposits:** turning the game's blue crystal noise on gave next to no blue crystal: it is first in the list of deep resources, where the last match wins (iron, brimstone and stone painted over it), only in the top 10 layers of the deep rock, and only above 0.715 of its noise. Its entry is now moved to the end of the list (after stone, so it wins where it matches), at any depth, above 0.6; the map's `noise.json` is still written with the game's own entry. Blue crystal could also not be carried, stored or cleaned up (bricktrons and stockpiles had no capacity for it): everything that can hold orange crystal can now hold as much blue crystal. When a map loads, the log lists how much of the deep rock each noise would take ("Deep rock noise shares").
- [ADD] **DirectTask:** select bricktrons and right-click a concrete task: a blueprint or demolish ghost, a tree of a tree harvest area, a block of a dig (mining, landscape, tunnel) area, a boulder, crystal or plant of a harvest area, or an item of a cleanup area. They start that very task at once. The game's right-click only sent them there (or made a new task of its own beside the area's) and took them out of their crew; now the area's task is given to them the way their project gives work, so they keep their crew and go back to its work afterwards. Several selected bricktrons on a task only one can do (a block, a tree) take the nearest free tasks of the same area within 6 blocks of the click; the task is reserved for them (TaskReservation). A right-click on anything else, or with the secondary or ternary action, works as before. The host decides; the client only sends a right-click on a blueprint as an order on the blueprint (the game sent the ground behind it).
- [FIX] A forge with a recipe of the mod in its queue (steel ingot) threw an error every time its queue changed: the forge shows a model of what it makes, looked up in a fixed table of the game's own products. A product that is not in it now shows no model.
- [FIX] **StockpileConsolidation / AutoCleanup:** workers picked things up and put them back in the same place over and over. On the way to the other stockpile (or to storage), the idle project gave the carrying worker the game's "store or drop" order, which replaced the chore: the load went into the nearest stockpile, the one it came from, and the next idle round planned the same move again. A worker carrying a load of an idle chore now keeps doing it.
- [FIX] **Metallurgy:** coal (and steel) could not be carried: the bricktrons' own capacity, and that of every other container, is set per resource and did not include the resource types coal and steel use, so coal made in the furnace stayed there. Everything that can hold raw iron can now hold as much coal, and everything that can hold iron as much steel; the log lists the containers changed.
- [CHANGE] **FastQuit:** Exit (from the main menu, or exit to desktop in a game) closes the game at once. The game's quit did the slow work twice: it first deactivated and destroyed every object of every scene and of the object pools one by one (each running its own clean-up, and every error on the way logged), and then ended the process with CloseMainWindow, which on Linux is SIGTERM, so Unity then shut down in full and unloaded every loaded object and asset again; all of it was thrown away with the process. Now the game's settings are saved (the mod's config is saved whenever it changes), a running autosave is allowed to finish (up to 30 seconds), and the process is ended right away.
- [ADD] **DebugMenu:** F8 (`[Debug] DebugMenuKey`) opens a debug menu for testing. Energy: +100, +500, +1000 or +5000 energy in the home crystal (an unnamed firefly stored in it, as brewing does). Bricktrons: +1 or +5 builders at the home crystal, heal every own bricktron. Resources: drop 10, 50 or 100 of any resource (logs, planks, stone, bricks, fibre, rope, fabric, raw iron, iron, cogs, orange and blue crystal, glass, coal, steel) on the ground where the camera looks (the log then tells, as "storage check", what the item holds, every own stockpile's capacity and room for it, and whether a worker can carry and store it). Enemies: +1 or +5 corruptrons where the camera looks (without a raid, so they act on their own), kill every enemy within 30 blocks of it. Invasion waves: next wave now, +1 or +5 minutes before the next wave, freeze and resume the wave timer, with the time left shown; the commands are carried out by the invasion server script on its next slow update and synced to clients the way the game syncs the wave timer. Host only; Esc closes it.
- [ADD] **ResourceReservation:** a worker reserves the resources it sets out to fetch. The game's search for something to pick up only looked at what lay there, not at who was already on the way, so several workers walked to the same loose item or to the last few items of a stockpile, and all but the first found nothing, failed their task and searched again from wherever they had walked to. Now a loose item is reserved whole by the worker going for it, and in a stockpile the worker reserves as many items of the kind as it will take (its bag room, or the task's limit); other workers only choose that stockpile while it holds unreserved items of what they look for, and otherwise fetch elsewhere. The reservation ends when the worker has picked up, starts another task, is gone, or after 30 seconds. Auto cleanup skips reserved items too. Host only.
- [FIX] **CopyPaste:** Ctrl+C also sounded the call to arms (the game's call to arms key is C and ignores Ctrl). The game's key actions now stand by while Ctrl is held with the copy or paste key.
- [ADD] **CraftLoopLimit:** a workshop's looping queue can loop until a stock limit. Next to the game's loop button in the workshop menu (shown whenever the workshop has a queue) a stockpile button turns the limit on and off (yellow and highlighted while on; turning it on turns looping on), and "-" and "+" buttons around it change it: 1 at a time under 10, then 5 under 50, then 10 (5 at first, at most 500). The menu only shows icons, so the buttons' tooltip tells the limit and the stock now. With a limit, a recipe only starts while the team's stockpiles and tool racks hold less than the limit of what it makes; when every recipe in the queue has enough, the workshop waits with its queue and starts again by itself once the stock drops below the limit (e.g. a furnace looping iron ingots with limit 5 keeps 5 iron in stock). The limit is per workshop, remembered while off, saved with it (an extra `loopLimit` property, ignored by the unmodded game) and synced to multiplayer clients; clearing the queue clears it.
- [ADD] **DropGearOnDeath:** a dying bricktron drops its gear (weapons, shields, helmets and hats, quivers, pouches and bags, artificer and alchemist gear) on the ground, where workers can pick it up again, instead of taking it into the corpse, where it disappeared. Gear the game conjured (magical tools, e.g. from respawning with tools) and aesthetic items still disappear, so dying cannot make gear. Host only.
- [FIX] **ArtificerHealing:** every healing bolt gave the error "Did not find target for syncEvent": the bolt was set to be destroyed on its hit, which happened before the game's hit event reached the clients (the host's own client too). It now ends like the artificer's other bolts.
- [ADD] **ArtificerHealing:** artificers heal by shooting. Instead of the staff's healing aura (which only healed while the artificer held position), an artificer shoots a green healing bolt at the most wounded bricktron of its team within its attack range (below 95% health), one at a time, with the same aiming, animation and cooldown as its attack. Each bolt heals 10% of the target's maximum health and deals no damage. Healing comes before attacking; an artificer carrying out a player's order is left alone, and a team mate it could not shoot at (out of sight or reach) is skipped for 3 seconds. While it shoots a bolt it counts as fighting, so its instinct does not replace the bolt with an attack. The staff stays in attack mode, also while holding position. Host only; the bolt and its trail are tinted green on every machine with the mod (the trail is a pooled object of its own, put back to its colours for ordinary shots).
- [ADD] **Metallurgy:** two new resources, coal and steel. Coal is mined from coal veins in the deep rock of every map, also existing maps and saves (the deep rock is not saved: every machine works it out from the map's seed when the map is opened, so the veins appear wherever nobody has dug yet), or burnt from logs in the furnace (Charcoal: 1 log and 1 heat give 4 coal). The veins are a terrain type of their own (61): iron ore's digging time and look with black specks (painted at load into the free cell next to the ore specks in the terrain's overlay texture), and a copy of iron's noise with other seeds, so veins are about as common as iron and never in the same place. The coal noise is left out of the map's `noise.json`, so a saved map stays as the game wrote it. Mines and tunnels treat coal like iron: it follows the iron setting, counts towards a digger's load and is carried to storage; coal left on the ground merges back into the terrain as coal. Coal is not found in stone. Steel is made at the forge (Steel ingot: 1 iron ingot and 4 coal, 8 work). Both have their own items (raw iron turned black, the iron ingot turned light silver), icons made from the game's iron icons (`tools/make_resource_icons.py`), and entries in the resource list, the crafting menus and the stockpiles (240 coal, 48 steel per pallet). They are the game's unused Clay and Terracotta resource types, which the stockpiles, saves and network already know; the Lua UI gets new values for them past the end of its resource and recipe lists. The recipes are made after all others, so existing recipe ids do not move. Saves with coal or steel need the mod.
- [CHANGE] **Upgrades:** the steel tier of every upgrade costs steel instead of iron (half as many steel as it took iron, and 2 steel added to Padded Gambeson). Without the Metallurgy feature the steel tier costs iron as before. Upgrade costs list coal and steel by name and icon.
- [ADD] **BlueCrystalDeposits:** blue crystal veins in the deep rock of every map. The game has them, but every preset turns them off and no menu turns them on; they are turned on after the preset is read, on every machine (each one generates the deep rock from the shared seed, so clients need the mod too). Mining and tunnel projects store the blue crystal they dig.
- [ADD] **RespawnCost:** a dead bricktron's firefly respawns for a flat 100 energy, instead of the price of the next new bricktron (100, plus 60 per bricktron over 6). A player's dead bricktron no longer carries a refund home (by default the game refunded the full price of a new bricktron, more than the respawn now costs), so the respawn really costs 100. Fireflies of AI factions keep their refund for the killer. New bricktrons keep the game's growing price. The firefly's status ("needs N energy") uses the flat price. Host only.
- [ADD] **SystemLog:** F9 (`[Debug] SystemLogKey`) opens a system log window with three tabs. Bricktrons: every bricktron of your faction (or all factions) with job, activity, current task, time on it and what it carries; idle ones in yellow, fighting in red, and in orange those on the same task for over two minutes, with a summary line. Problems: errors and warnings of the game, the mod and Harmony, and bricktron tasks that failed (path failed, could not reach, worker failed...), newest first, repeats counted; click one to see all of it with its stack trace. Log: the mod's own log lines. It refreshes once a second while open; Esc closes it. Tasks only exist on the host, so a multiplayer client sees jobs and activities only.
- [ADD] **TaskFlow:** less dead time between tasks, and transport thought into them. A finished work task waits 1 tick instead of 10, and the worker may look for work again at once instead of after 0.05 s. Materials for a blueprint or a workshop come from the stockpile that makes the shortest trip worker -> stockpile -> destination, instead of the stockpile nearest the worker. A worker whose bag is full after digging, felling a tree or harvesting stores it as the last step of that task, instead of waiting for a separate idle round that only came once every project had turned it down.
- [FIX] **MoveStructure:** after the old building was demolished, the workers never built the new one: its materials were dropped on the ground, and workers do not fetch loose items for a blueprint. The materials now go straight into the new blueprint, so it only needs the building work; only what does not fit is dropped. Pending moves in a save also remember the new blueprint.
- [ADD] **Upgrades:** researched tiers show on the bricktrons' gear. Swords and poleaxes take the highest blade tier (Sharpened or Heavy Blades), bows and crossbows the highest ranged tier (Fletching, Steady Aim, Winch), helmets, kettle hats, kepis and caps the highest armour tier (Reinforced Helmet, Chainmail, Padded Gambeson, Warded Plate), shields the Shield Rims tier. Iron is a little warmer, steel brighter and cooler, crystal blue with a glow. The game's own models and textures are kept: their materials are tinted, and gear gets its own materials back when it is unequipped. Gear updates when a tier is researched, loaded from a save or received from the host.
- [CHANGE] **LargeStockpile:** the large stockpile shows its content with the stockpile's own models in their own size, instead of the 2 x 2 display stretched 1.5 x. Raw stone, raw iron, crystals and plant fibre get one block-sized crate per block, filling up like the stockpile's crate; bricks, logs and planks lie in full size around a half block, every other layer turned; iron and glass ingots lie in two crossed layers (36 when full); ropes, fabric and cogs get one per block in two layers. A mixed large stockpile gets a column of the same pieces per block. Other resources (bombs, wards...) keep the stretched display.
- [ADD] **Upgrades:** two new buildings in the build menu (crafting group, 4 x 3 blocks): the smithy (10 bricks, 6 planks, 2 iron) for weapon upgrades and the armoury (10 planks, 4 bricks, 2 fabric) for armour upgrades. Select one to open its upgrade window: one row per upgrade with its icon, the tier researched, what the next tier does and what it costs (red when the stockpiles hold too little), and a Research button; the queue (up to 5) is listed below it. Every upgrade has three tiers (iron, steel, crystal), each a crafting recipe: a worker brings the materials and works on it, and when it is done the tier works at once for every bricktron of the team, also new ones. Smithy: Sharpened Blades (+15/30/50% melee damage), Heavy Blades (+25/50/75% damage to blocks and buildings), Fletching (+15/30/50% arrow damage for archers), Steady Aim (-25/50/75% spread on archers' shots without a clear line of sight), Winch (-10/20/30% crossbow reload time). Armoury, for soldiers (any job but builder): Chainmail (-10/20/30% melee damage taken), Padded Gambeson (-10/20/30% arrow and bolt damage taken), Reinforced Helmet (+10/20/30% max health), Shield Rims (-10/20/30% stamina per blocked hit, +15/30/45 degrees block arc), Warded Plate (-5/10/15% magic and explosion damage taken). Tier costs go from iron, planks, rope and fabric (tier 1) over more iron, bricks and cogs (tier 2) to blue crystal (tier 3). The tiers are saved with the faction (an extra `upgrades` property, ignored by the unmodded game) and synced to multiplayer clients. The game already has a building called Forge, so the weapon building is the smithy.
- [CHANGE] **Market:** the market's model and building code now use shared helpers (`Building/BoxModel.cs`, `Building/CustomBuilding.cs`) that the smithy and armoury use as well; the market itself is unchanged.
- [ADD] **AutoCleanup:** idle workers pick up loose items lying on the ground and carry them to storage, without a cleanup zone. They look within 15 blocks (`[AutoCleanup] Radius`, also in the Castle Story Plus settings) of the home crystal, any own stockpile and themselves, nearest item first. Each item is claimed by one worker. Any real task comes first, and auto cleanup comes before stockpile consolidation. Host only; not in the world editor.
